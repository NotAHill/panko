## How to check an item

Decide, like a careful native-speaker teacher would:
1. **Real and correct?** Is it genuine {{language}} with this meaning? Are the spelling and romanisations
   plausible? Is the gloss right, not too narrow or wrong (a general verb glossed only as "to cook")?
2. **Where is it used?** One of {{regions}}, or a short combination ("north and Fes"). Say if it is urban or rural,
   or old-fashioned. {{#mainstream}}The mainstream standard is {{mainstream}}: if most speakers there would say
   something else, give that `usual_word` (the word + romanisation).{{/mainstream}}
3. **Register**: neutral, informal, slang, polite, rude{{#standard}}, {{standard}}-ish (understood but bookish in
   speech){{/standard}}, or a loan{{#loans}} ({{loans}}…){{/loans}}.
4. **Sensitive?** Anything ethnic, crude or likely to offend in some company.

Verdicts:
- `ok`: fine as is.
- `fix`: the right word with a wrong detail; correct it. A fix needs evidence or a plain slip (a typo, a spelling
  that doesn't match its romanisation). When only your memory disagrees with what a teaching source (a teacher,
  a textbook, a lesson) says, a grammar rule, a form, a sense, the source wins: use `ok`, and if most speakers say
  something else, give it as `usual_word` with a `learner_note`. A teaching source's word is `doubtful` only when
  evidence speaks against it, not because you'd have said it differently.
- `regional`: real but tied to a region (say which).
- `uncommon`: real but rare, old-fashioned or bookish.
- `doubtful`: you couldn't confirm it.
- `drop`: not {{language}}, or wrong.

`confidence`: high (evidence says so), medium (indirect evidence), low (memory, unsure). An honest "unsure,
probably pan-regional" is fine.

`learner_note`: one short dictionary-style sentence shown on the card, **only** when the item is regional, rural,
old-fashioned, slangy, rude or not the usual word ("{{ex_learner_note}}").
Empty for ordinary words. Nothing about sources or doubt.

Check example sentences too: natural, everyday language (not a calque of {{gloss_language}}), using the item in
this sense, with a translation that says what the sentence says.
